Most of SEO is a build concern, not a design one. What the design owns is on this artboard: the heading structure, the indexable text, the title and description that appear in results, and the card that appears when someone shares a link. Get these wrong and no amount of technical work rescues the page.

Rules for the card
Exactly 1200 × 630. Anything else gets cropped unpredictably.
Keep text inside a 60px safe margin — some clients crop to 1.91:1 tighter than others.
One sentence, max 16 characters per line at this size. No paragraph.
Generate one per page. A single site-wide card kills click-through on shared deep links.

Indexable-text audit
Every headline is real text, not an image.
The 3D objects and dashboards are decorative — mark them aria-hidden.
Practice tab panels ship in the HTML, hidden with CSS — not injected on click.
Team and case-study photos need real alt text describing the content, not “team photo”.

Not a design problem — but don't skip it

These decide most of the ranking outcome and none of them live in this canvas. Whoever builds the site owns them.

·Server-rendered HTML. A client-only render is a ranking handicap for a site this small.
·Core Web Vitals — the hero solid and the grid are cheap, but self-host the fonts and preload the two weights actually used.
·Sitemap, robots.txt, and a canonical host (pick www or apex, redirect the other).
·A Google Business Profile for [City] — for a firm this size it will out-earn most on-page work.
·Case studies are the pages that rank for buying intent. Publish them as their own URLs, not tabs.
